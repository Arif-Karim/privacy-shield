Privacy Shield — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-08-03

Privacy Shield is a browser extension that warns you before you submit your email address or phone number to a website that may share or sell your data. This page explains what the extension does and does not do with your information.

What the extension accesses

  • Page content on sites you visit. The extension reads the current page's HTML to detect form fields that look like they collect an email address or phone number, and to look for a link to that site's own privacy policy. This runs locally in your browser.
  • The current site's domain name. Used to check whether the site already has a public rating from ToS;DR, a crowdsourced privacy/terms-of-service rating project.
  • The site's own privacy policy page. If ToS;DR has no rating, the extension fetches the privacy policy page the site itself links to. The domain name, together with the text of that public privacy policy page, is sent to the extension's own backend service (see “Third parties contacted” below) to be analyzed for language indicating the site shares or sells personal data.

What the extension does NOT do

  • It does not collect, store, or transmit the actual information you type into forms (your email address, phone number, name, etc.).
  • It does not track your browsing history or send a record of the sites you visit anywhere. Only the domain of a site with a detected email/phone form, plus that site's public privacy-policy text, is sent for analysis — and only for the purpose of rating that site.
  • It does not run any advertising or third-party analytics.

Third parties contacted

  • api.tosdr.org — queried with only the domain name of the site you're currently on (e.g. example.com), to retrieve a public rating. No personal data is sent.
  • The website you're visiting, and its own privacy policy page — the extension fetches this page's public text directly from the site. No personal data is sent in this request beyond a normal page load.
  • The Privacy Shield backend service (a Cloudflare Worker operated by the developer) — when a site has no ToS;DR rating, the domain name and the public privacy-policy text are sent here to be classified. The backend caches the resulting rating by domain so the same site does not have to be re-analyzed for every user. It does not receive anything you type into forms.
  • Anthropic (Claude API) — the backend service forwards the privacy-policy text (or, if none is found, just the domain name) to Anthropic's Claude model to produce the rating. Anthropic processes this request under its own API terms; the text sent is the site's public privacy policy, not your personal data.

Local storage

The extension does not persist a history of sites you've visited or their ratings on your device. A short-lived, in-memory record of the current tab's result is kept only so the popup can display it, and this is cleared automatically whenever the tab navigates to a new page. On the backend service, only the computed rating for a domain (green/yellow/red plus supporting reasons) is cached, keyed by domain name, so repeat visitors don't trigger a fresh analysis; this cache holds no information about who requested it.

"Report an issue" feature

If you click "Report an issue" in the popup, the extension prepares a pre-filled email (page URL, whether a form/privacy link was detected, and the last rating shown) and opens it in your own email client. Nothing is sent automatically — you see the full contents and choose whether to send it, exactly like any other email you write.
